⚠️ Protocol: Individual Proposals, Team Projects

To ensure every student has a rigorous grasp of the project scope, we follow a strict submission protocol:

  • Proposals are INDIVIDUAL: Even if you have formed a team (max {{MAX_TEAM_SIZE}}), EACH member must submit their own PDF proposal.
  • What You Can Share: Team members may copy/paste the same Problem Statement and Background/Related Work sections.
  • What Must Be Unique: Your Methodology and Milestones must reflect your specific role and technical contributions to the effort.
  • Co-Authoring: You may submit shared Interim Reports, Presentations, and Final Codebases/Reports ONLY AFTER your proposal has been approved and your team has been officially registered.

Methodological Pillars (The How)

This curriculum engages the five pillars of modern artificial intelligence and machine learning—language, vision, reinforcement learning, trustworthiness, and applied systems—to transition students from theoretical principles to the construction of robust, intelligent agents.

Natural Language [NL]

Focuses on representation, extraction, reasoning, trust, and longitudinal modeling of meaning in text and multimodal language artifacts.

Computer Vision [CV]

Focuses on structured perception, hierarchical interpretation, and explainable visual understanding, especially where vision is tightly coupled with language.

Reinforcement Learning [RL]

Focuses on learning, reasoning, and decision-making under uncertainty, emphasizing interpretability, causal structure, and semantic representations.

Secure & Trustworthy [ST]

Focuses on ensuring reliability, robustness, interpretability, and alignment of learning and reasoning systems in high-stakes environments.

Applied AI Systems [OA]

Serves as the translational and integrative layer connecting foundational AI research to real-world deployment, constraints, and performance considerations.

Project Category Tracks (The What)

Your project must align with one of these four foundational AI application tracks:

Track 1: Search, Games, and Planning

Covers problems where an agent must navigate a complex state space to achieve a goal, often against an adversary or under strict resource constraints. While classical search algorithms (A*, Minimax, MCTS) are foundational, projects frequently integrate Reinforcement Learning [RL] or heuristic neural models to handle high-dimensional state spaces.

Track 2: Knowledge, Logic, and Reasoning

Focuses on representing structured knowledge through ontologies, knowledge graphs, and logical rules. Projects here often leverage Secure & Trustworthy [ST] or Natural Language [NL] methodologies to ensure that automated reasoning systems remain transparent, verifiable, and grounded.

Track 3: Machine Learning & Probabilistic Models

Encompasses statistical learning, spanning from Bayesian networks and deep neural architectures to foundation model fine-tuning. A critical emphasis is placed on Applied AI Systems [OA] engineering, bridging theoretical modeling with rigorous validation.

Track 4: Perception and Agentic Systems

Deals with autonomous agents that perceive and act within complex environments through multimodal sensors (text, vision, audio). This track heavily integrates Computer Vision [CV] and Natural Language [NL] to ground unstructured perceptual streams into actionable agentic policies.

Proposal Document Format

Your proposal must be a concise 1–1.5 page PDF structured into the following five required sections:

1. Introduction

  • Problem Statement: Clearly describe the AI/ML problem you intend to solve.
  • Goal: What specific capability or improvement will your system accomplish?
  • Context & Dataset: Define your data source or domain. Clarify whether this is an investigation of novelty (Research) or utility (Application).

2. Background & Related Work

  • Summarize relevant prior work and foundational literature.
  • Requirement: At least 3 citations/references.
  • Requirement: At least one archival/peer-reviewed source (ACM, IEEE, NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, etc.).

3. Methodology

  • Baselines: What existing method or model architecture will serve as your comparative baseline? (e.g., standard fine-tuned baseline, rule-based agent).
  • Proposed Approach: What novel algorithm, architecture, or system integration will you implement?
  • Technical Specifics: Detail your modeling framework, training objective, algorithms, and software libraries. Avoid vague assertions.

4. Evaluation Plan

  • Quantitative Metrics: Accuracy, F1-score, BLEU, ROC-AUC, reward convergence, or latency benchmarks.
  • Qualitative Assessment: Error analysis, sample generation audits, or interpretive case studies.
  • Validation Rigor: Train/test splits, cross-validation protocols, and statistical hypothesis testing (e.g., paired t-tests).

5. Milestones & Timeline

  • List 3–6 dated objectives (e.g., {{DATE_MILESTONE_EX}}: Baseline Architecture & Data Pipeline Implemented).
